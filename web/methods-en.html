PapaMap — methods & honesty

Changing tables a dad can reach. The worldwide numbers on this page come from the nightly build. The eight-city sample is from 26 Jul 2026 and stays that way.

What this map is

PapaMap shows places in Europe, Australia, New Zealand, the United States, Canada and Japan where OpenStreetMap knows about a changing table (changing_table=yes or limited). The colour says whether a dad can reach it. The map only reads OSM and writes nothing. If something is wrong, you fix it on OSM, through the links on each pin.

The idea came from reading Invisible Women by Caroline Criado Perez.

It is not a ranking of which city has the best toilets. Why that can't be done from open data is further down.

What the colours mean

Only objects with changing_table=yes or limited get a pin (no only counts in the stats). The colour comes from changing_table:location. The value is split on ;, trimmed, lowercased and compared as a whole token, never as a substring, because female_toilet contains male.

  • Green: one token is male_toilet, unisex_toilet, dedicated_room, room, wheelchair_toilet, sales_area or outdoor. A dad can get there.
  • Red: otherwise, if one token is female_toilet. The table exists, but only in the women's room.
  • Grey: everything else. No location tag, or only free text. Nobody has written down the room yet.

An object with a centralkey tag (other than no) is left off the map when the key locks the whole door: access=centralkey, a table in the wheelchair toilet, or nothing in the tags saying the key is for one cubicle only. A block with open men's and women's rooms and a key-locked accessible cubicle (wheelchair:access=centralkey, or male=yes and female=yes) stays on the map, grey until someone records the room. You only get a Euro or RADAR key with proof of disability, so most dads don't get through that door.

The blue ring: play area

A pin with a blue ring also has a play area. The With play area chip filters down to those. A place counts if OSM has one of these tags:

  • kids_area:indoor=yes or designated
  • kids_area=yes, indoor or designated (the most common tag, it doesn't say indoor or outdoor)
  • leisure=indoor_play
  • leisure=playground together with indoor=yes

outdoor, no and limited don't count. An explicit kids_area:indoor=no beats a bare kids_area=yes.

The ring is a badge, not a fourth colour. A place without these tags doesn't "have no play area", OSM just says nothing about it, and that is true for almost every place. So there is no grey here.

Many more places pass this rule than have a pin. Only 111 of them carry a changing_table tag at all. At 701 more, nobody has answered the first question: is there a changing table here? The map can't colour those, so they get an empty blue ring and their own chip, Play area only, off by default. If you go there with your kid anyway, you know the answer. Clicking a ring opens MapComplete on exactly that question.

How to fix a grey pin

Grey means one answer is missing: where is the table? Anyone with a free OSM account can answer that on site in under a minute.

  • MapComplete (any phone, in the browser): “Answer on MapComplete” on a grey pin opens the same object in PapaMap's own MapComplete theme. MapComplete warns once about an unofficial theme, that's fine. Log in with your OSM account and answer the changing-table questions, including the one about the room. The answer goes straight into OpenStreetMap and shows up here after the next nightly update.
  • StreetComplete (Android): has a changing-table quest, off by default. Settings → Quest selection and display order, search for “changing table”, tick it. It only asks yes/no. For the room question use MapComplete.

The edits run under your own OSM account and help every app that reads OpenStreetMap, not just this one.

How to add a missing place

PapaMap stores nothing itself, so adding a place means editing OpenStreetMap. The + Add a place button on the map opens the right editor at your current view.

A public toilet is missing entirely

Take PapaMap's MapComplete theme (the first link in the dialog). Log in, tap the map where the toilet is, pick “a public toilet” and answer the questions, including the ones about the changing table and the room.

A café, shop or restaurant has a table nobody mapped

The quickest way is PapaMap's MapComplete theme, the one the dialog's second button opens too: from zoom 16 it also shows cafés, restaurants, shops, libraries, museums, pools and stations that carry no changing-table tag yet. Tap the place, answer the changing-table question and then the room question. If the place isn't shown, or you'd rather use OSM's own editor, the steps below still work.

The place is almost certainly in OSM already, only the changing_table tag is missing. In OSM's own editor (iD) that takes about two minutes:

  1. Create a free account on openstreetmap.org (one email is enough) and log in.
  2. Find the place and click Edit. The place is a point or a building outline. Click it, its tags appear on the left.
  3. Expand Tags at the bottom and add with +:
    • changing_table = yes
    • changing_table:location = where it is, from the documented value list: male_toilet, female_toilet, unisex_toilet, wheelchair_toilet, dedicated_room, room, sales_area or outdoor. In several rooms: separate with semicolons (male_toilet;female_toilet).
  4. Press Save, write a short comment (“add changing table location”), upload. Live on OSM within a minute, on PapaMap after the next nightly update.

Please only tag what you have seen yourself. If you don't know the room, changing_table=yes alone is still useful: the place becomes a grey pin, and the next dad answers the room question.

Why there is no city ranking

The obvious bigger project would be a male-to-female toilet ratio per city, the number the “potty parity” debate is about. I checked before building whether OSM can give that number. It can't. A sample across eight city cores from 26 Jul 2026:

Only 29.9% of mapped toilets carry any gender tag, 8.5% carry both. The tags are yes/no flags, never counts, and cubicle counts per gender are zero in all eight cities. And a tagging rate measures the mapper community, not the city. A ranking would grade OSM contributors, not toilets. So this site sticks to what can be measured.

Where the tables are

changing_table:location is the only open vocabulary I know of that records which room a changing table is in. It is rare: 3,659 of 77,000 changing_table objects worldwide, 4.8%. Where it exists it is lopsided (taginfo, 26 Jul 2026): 485 tables only in the women's room, 71 only in the men's room, roughly 7:1. 289 are in both rooms.

The sample is small and mappers choose what they record, so don't read too much into the exact ratio. The direction is the same as everywhere else: changing tables hang in the women's room by default. This map shows where the data is missing, and every grey pin has an edit button.

Privacy

A static site. It sets no cookies and runs no analytics. The map tiles come from OpenStreetMap's tile servers, so those requests fall under the OSMF privacy policy. Links to MapComplete, StreetComplete or openstreetmap.org take you to their sites, where you edit under your own OSM account. Formal pages: Impressum · Datenschutzerklärung (German).
